## 2026-10-20 — MUVERA: multi-vector retrieval through one vector (17 new, 12 updated)

Multi-vector models like [[ColBERT]] score with MaxSim, which [[Approximate Nearest Neighbor Search|ANN indexes]] can't search directly. [[MUVERA]] folds each document's token vectors into one fixed-dimensional encoding whose inner product approximates MaxSim, retrieves on that, then reranks with the real vectors. [[MUVERA]] now covers the [[Google]] paper's construction and its comparison with [[PLAID]]: Recall@k 10% higher and latency 90% lower on average over six [[BEIR]] datasets, with no win on [[MS MARCO]]. It also covers implementations in [[Weaviate Vector DB]], [[Qdrant Vector DB]] via [[FastEmbed]], and an [[OpenSearch]] plugin. Its benchmarks on three small corpora keep 87–97% of exact quality after rerank; [[Elastic]]'s approach, average vectors, keeps 28–52%. All figures are the authors' own.

## 2026-10-20 — Click residuals for finding underperforming queries (2 new, 6 updated)

When a query gets plenty of searches but few clicks, [[Clicks Residual|click residual]] puts a number on the shortfall: actual clicks minus attempts × the site's average CTR. Ranking queries by residual brings high-impact failures to the top, where a plain low-CTR list would bury them in the long tail. [[Clicks Residual]] now covers [[Walter Underwood]]'s original definition with his [[Netflix]] cases. It also covers [[Charlie Hull]]'s e-commerce extensions: per-category CTR baselines, exit rate and business value for prioritising, and query clustering to catch whole failing families such as misspellings. The metric shows *that* a query fails, not *why*. Sourced from Underwood's 2022 blog post and [[Click Residuals - A Clever Way to Find Underperforming Queries]].

**Corrections**
- [[Click Residual - A Query Success Metric]] credited the metric to "Ted Underwood" and linked a dead Medium URL. It is by [[Walter Underwood]], published on his own blog in 2022.
- That note's position-discounted formula and "running shoes" example are not in the source. It now gives Underwood's exp = att × ctr, res = clk − exp.
- [[Search Observability]] called click residual "position-bias corrected". It uses a single average CTR baseline.
