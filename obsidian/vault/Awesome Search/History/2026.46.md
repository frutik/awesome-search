## 2026-11-09 — Macro, micro and weighted F1 (2 new, 10 updated)

F1 for a multi-class task depends on how the per-class scores are averaged. [[Macro and Micro Averaging]] explains the options. Macro counts every class equally, which exposes rare classes that score poorly. Micro pools every decision, and in single-label multiclass it equals accuracy. Macro-F1 is the mean of the per-class F1 scores, not the harmonic mean of macro precision and macro recall, so two reported "macro-F1" numbers may not be comparable. Existing figures now link to it, such as the [[Fine-Tuning Qwen3 Embeddings for Product Category Classification|Qwen3 category classifier]] (0.836 macro-F1 against 87.91% accuracy on six classes) and [[Etsy]]'s judge cascade. Sourced from the [[scikit-learn - Metrics and Scoring|scikit-learn user guide]].

## 2026-11-09 — Why nDCG misleads eCommerce search (1 new, 9 updated)

Is nDCG the right number to optimise for product search? [[Andreas Wagner]] of [[searchHub]] argues not, in two LinkedIn posts now combined as [[Why nDCG Misleads eCommerce Search]]. Its log discount is assumed rather than measured, nDCG@5 or @10 ignores how far shoppers scroll, and product grids make attention drop in steps at row and viewport boundaries that differ between desktop and mobile, which a smooth curve cannot model. Its tie to revenue also weakens once images, prices, brands and promotions drive clicks. [[NDCG]] gains an eCommerce-limitations section and [[Position Bias]] now covers grid-shaped examination. [[Andrew Kornilov]]'s reply is recorded: teams use nDCG because nothing clearer is implementable.

## 2026-11-09 — A glossary for LLM-as-a-judge terms (1 new, 4 updated)

Agreement, self-consistency and calibration are three different claims about an LLM judge. [[LLM as Judge Vocabulary]] defines each term: the four [[Levels of Judge Agreement|levels of agreement]], inter-judge and ordinal-versus-binary agreement, qrels, learned adjudication, distilled students, two senses of calibration, and juries. Two 2026 arXiv preprints add the evidence. On conversational-RAG context sufficiency, three-label κ of 0.38–0.49 rose to 0.59–0.79 once collapsed to answer-or-abstain, and reading "no gold document" as insufficient evidence lost most of its precision as retrievers improved. CADRE's trained verifier over three specialist judges beat an oracle picking any correct expert label on 18,710 held-out e-commerce pairs, yet letting the judges see each other cost up to 1.76 accuracy points. Both are unreviewed preprints.
