## 2026-11-01 — The search frontier, second half of 2026 (1 new, 2 updated)

In the second half of 2026, the leading edge of search is less about finding candidates than about judging them. [[Frontier of Search 2026 Part 2]] picks up where [[Frontier of Search 2026]] left off in June and sets out three new fronts. First, the [[LLM as Judge|LLM judge]], made cheap through [[Staged Judging|cascades]] and examined through [[Levels of Judge Agreement|agreement levels]] and [[Judge Calibration|calibration]]. Second, decision models such as [[Jev]] that return a [[Calibrated Relevance Probability|calibrated probability]]. Third, cheaper reranking with [[BlitzRank]] and [[Fast but Not Late Interaction]]. It also follows commoditization ([[MUVERA]]), agent memory and [[Query Routing|routing]], and growing doubt about reported figures ([[Benchmark Contamination]]). Most figures it cites are vendor- or author-reported.

## 2026-10-30 — Golden sets and the NDCG that flatters them (4 new, 7 updated)

A golden set of graded judgments can score a search system at NDCG 1 while users are unhappy, simply because the corpus holds few relevant results for the query. [[Doug Turnbull]] argues for narrower evals: [[NDCG-m]], named for [[Max Irwin]], normalises against a fixed all-max-grade ideal so corpus coverage shows in the score; [[NDCG-f]] scores labeled results only, as a regression check when labels are incomplete; and [[Rank-Biased Overlap]] measures how far results moved. The post reports an average NDCG-m@10 of about 0.77 on [[WANDS Dataset|WANDS]] and closer to 0.93 on [[Amazon ESCI Dataset|ESCI]]. The first two extend [[NDCG]]; the third compares rankings without labels.

## 2026-10-30 — Fast but Not Late Interaction: rerankers with cached passages (3 new, 5 updated)

A [[Cross-Encoder]] re-encodes every passage for every query, while [[Late Interaction]] avoids that with a fixed token-level MaxSim comparison. A third design, [[Fast but Not Late Interaction]], encodes passages offline into compressed token states and lets a learned decoder read them alongside the query, with the compression ratio adjustable from 1x to 128x at inference. The concept note places it against cross-encoders, late interaction and [[MUVERA]], and [[Lychee-Reranker]] covers the open model family built on it. Sourced from the authors' technical report; its cost figures are analytical estimates rather than measured latency, and on [[BEIR]] the largest model trails Qwen3-Reranker-8B (see [[Qwen3 Embedding Series]]).
