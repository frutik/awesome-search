### 2026-09-26 — Query routing, from web verticals to RAG (12 new, 17 updated)

Routing a query means choosing where it goes — a data source, a retriever, an embedding model, or how much retrieval to do at all — and web search solved an early version in 2009, deciding which verticals a query should trigger ([[Sources of Evidence for Vertical Selection]]). [[Query Routing]] now traces that line into RAG: [[Adaptive Retrieval|complexity routing]], [[RouterRetriever - Routing over a Mixture of Expert Embedding Models|domain-expert encoders]], [[LTRR - Learning To Rank Retrievers for LLMs|ranking retrievers]], [[Efficient Federated Search for RAG using Lightweight Routing|federated source selection]] and [[DAT - Dynamic Alpha Tuning for Hybrid Retrieval in RAG|per-query hybrid weights]]. Two findings stand out: a complexity router only 55% accurate still matched always-multi-step answers at under half the time, and randomly chosen sources scored below no retrieval on MMLU. All figures come from the papers' own benchmarks.

### 2026-09-26 — Query routers for RAG, cheapest first (4 new, 5 updated)

A RAG app with several sources has to decide, per question, where to look — a product catalog, a returns FAQ, a keyword index, a web search. [[Build an Advanced RAG App - Query Routing]] by [[Roger Oriol]] catalogues what a router can choose between and six ways to decide, from LLM selectors to plain keyword rules, walked through in [[LlamaIndex]]. [[Query Routing - Direct Queries to the Right Source]] from [[Ailog]] chains them into a cascade — regex first, embeddings next, an LLM only for what remains — and adds multi-route and user-context routing. [[Query Routing]] now covers router choices, the cascade and multi-route. Both are tutorials: no routing accuracy is measured, and Ailog's thresholds are example values.

### 2026-09-26 — Routing queries past reciprocal rank fusion (2 new, 6 updated)

[[Reciprocal Rank Fusion]] is the default merge in [[Hybrid Search]], yet on some queries it ranks below sparse-only or dense-only retrieval. [[Andrei Cristea - Qdrant Vector Search and Hybrid Routing]] describes [[Qdrant]]'s answer: a small classifier that sends each query to the sparse retriever, the dense one, or RRF. Trained on about 230,000 labelled queries — where fusion was the right call for only 3% — it beat hybrid RRF on relevance, hit@1 and [[NDCG]] on a held-out set, while an earlier LLM router kept collapsing back to RRF. [[Query Routing]] and [[Query Classification]] now carry the case. From a five-minute [[Haystack EU]] lightning talk; no figures for the gains were given.

## 2026-09-21 — Jev's calibration claim, from the evaluation side (2 new, 5 updated)

A model whose selling point is a calibrated probability invites one question: calibrated to
what? [[Jev and the Return of AI-ML Engineering]] puts that question to [[Jev]] and takes apart
the four properties its launch leads with, arguing three of them — structured outputs, low
latency, low cost — already come off the shelf, since for small prefills the cost and latency gap
between autoregressive and non-autoregressive models is negligible. That leaves calibration,
where [[Han-chung Lee]] reports high [[Expected Calibration Error|calibration error]] over a coin
toss, two dice and three UCI datasets, and suspects the working demonstrations owe more to the
base model than to [[Reinforcement Learning for Calibrated Decisions|RLCD]]. The experiments are
described, not published; his own figures, he notes, establish nothing about predictive value.
